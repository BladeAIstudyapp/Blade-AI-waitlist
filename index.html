How does Shakespeare present ambition in Macbeth?

34 marks GCSE English Literature
Essay Feedback Improve
Show annotations
AO1 Clear argument & understanding AO2 Analysis of methods AO3 Context & ideas

In Macbeth, Shakespeare presents ambition as a force that can drive a man to greatness but ultimately leads to his downfall. Through Macbeth's gradual corruption and Banquo's moral clarity, Shakespeare explores both the allure and the danger of unchecked ambition. AO1

At the start of the play, Macbeth is portrayed as a noble and loyal thane, respected by others for his bravery in battle. However, his encounter with the witches plants the seed of ambition in his mind. Their prophecy that he will be king sparks a desire within him that he tries to resist. Shakespeare presents “vaulting ambition” as something Macbeth knows is dangerous, showing his initial awareness of its risks. AO2

As the play progresses, Macbeth's ambition grows and begins to control him. He allows Lady Macbeth to persuade him to murder Duncan, revealing how ambition can cloud judgement and lead to evil actions. Shakespeare uses dark imagery and violent language to show Macbeth's inner turmoil and his descent into guilt and paranoia. AO1

In contrast, Banquo is presented as ambitious but with integrity. He also hears the witches' prophecies, but he questions their truth and does not act on them. His scepticism shows that Shakespeare values moral strength over power. AO3
